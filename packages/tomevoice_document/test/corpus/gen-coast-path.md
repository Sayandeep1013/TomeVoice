A Week on the Coast Path
========================

*Notes written up each evening, in the order the days came.*

Day One: Lantern Cove
---------------------

We left the station at nine and were on the cliffs by ten. The path is
well marked, with a white acorn on every gate, and the first miles are
easy going. By noon we could see **Lantern Cove** below us, its harbour
wall curled like a hand around six small boats.[^tide]

> “Mind the tide,” said the woman at the café. “It comes in faster than
> you think, and it doesn’t wait for walkers.”

What we carried:

- a map, a compass and the tide tables;
- two litres of water each;
- far too many sandwiches.

* * *

## Day Two: The Salt Road

The second day was longer. We followed the old salt road inland, past
the ruined chapel and over the moor, and came down to the sea again at
dusk. Distances, from the guidebook:

| Stage         | Miles | Climb (ft) |
|---------------|------:|-----------:|
| Lantern Cove  |     4 |        350 |
| The Salt Road |     9 |        780 |
| Gull Point    |     6 |        420 |

The guidebook’s [website](https://example.org/coast-path) has the full
list. Its advice for the moor is short:

1. Keep the wall on your left.
2. Do not trust the cairns.
3. If the mist comes down, stop.

## Day Three: Gull Point

We woke to rain and walked out of it by eleven. The last stretch to Gull
Point is the finest on the whole path: high, bare and loud with birds.
We sat on the headland until the light went, and nobody said much.

[^tide]: The tide tables were an hour out all week; nobody knew why.
