## CHAPTER I.
CHAPTER I.
THE SHOP ON THE BRIDGE
In the spring of 1891 a boy named Tomas came to Zürich with one letter in his pocket and no money at all.
The letter was for Herr Albrecht Vogel, clockmaker, whose shop stood on the bridge over the Limmat.
“You are late,” said Herr Vogel, reading it twice.
“Three days late.
Can you sweep?”
Tomas could sweep.
He swept the shop, the stairs and the little room at the back where the clocks were mended, and by the evening he had learnt the names of forty clocks: the cuckoo clock from the Black Forest, the brass ship’s clock, and the tall clock from Lyon that struck the hours in French,
or so Herr Vogel said.
## CHAPTER II.
CHAPTER II.
THE CLOCK FROM ATHENS
In June a crate arrived from Greece.
On its side, in a careful hand, someone had written Αθήνα, and under it, in German, Vorsicht!—handle with care.
Inside was a clock unlike any other in the shop.
Its face showed the hours in Greek letters, Α to ΙΒ, and a small painted ship sailed across it every minute.
“It is broken,” said Herr Vogel.
“It has been broken for fifty years.
Nobody in Athens could mend it, and nobody in Paris either.”
Tomas looked at it for a long time… then he asked for a lamp, a pair of tweezers and a bowl of café au lait.
## CHAPTER III.
CHAPTER III.
THE SHIP SAILS AGAIN
It took him nine days.
On the tenth the little ship moved, slowly at first, then steadily, across the painted sea.
Herr Vogel said nothing at all.
He put on his coat and his hat, went out, and came back with a sign, which he hung in the window: Vogel & Lehrling, Uhrmacher.
Clockmakers.
The mending was worth 120 francs, which Tomas thought a fortune, and which Herr Vogel, who had been naïve about apprentices before, thought rather cheap.
