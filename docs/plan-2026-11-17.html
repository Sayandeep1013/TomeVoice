3. Web novels: chapters and downloads

Your notes here come down to three root causes.

A1 Any chapter you tap opens where you stopped (the “very bad bug”) Done · 1.4.2

Cause, confirmed. When you leave a book while it is reading or paused, it keeps a “reading in the background” session; for a daily listener that is nearly always. The novel page passes the chapter you tapped to the reader correctly. The reader's controller then sees the background session and puts the voice's place over your choice (reader_controller.dart:84-99). The novel page already skips this when you pick a chapter (web_reader.dart:166), but the controller doesn't know you picked one.

It also hits a bookmark, a search result, an Updates entry, and Contents after the voice moved on.

Fix. Tell the controller “this place was chosen”. Then it keeps your chapter, saves the background session's place, and doesn't play. The Focus / Back-to-reading button stays the way back to the voice, as you said. Test: a background session at chapter 5, open chapter 1, and the reader must be at chapter 1, silent.

A2 Tap chapter 1 (already downloaded) and it hangs on “Fetching chapter 4…” Done · 1.4.2

Cause, confirmed. Before showing anything, the reader waits until it has downloaded the next three chapters (web_reader.dart:313, with _ahead = 3). It fetches them one after another, each allowed up to 90 seconds. If one fails (rate limit, Cloudflare check, timeout), it says it couldn't open the chapter, though chapter 1 is on the phone. This fetching also runs beside the download queue, so the two fight over the same site, which makes stalls more likely.

Fix. Open the chapter you tapped at once. Fetch it only if it's missing. Fetch the next chapters afterwards, quietly, through the download queue, and never fail the page because of them.

A3 The download spinner never ends; Cancel shows the chapters were already downloaded Done · 1.4.2

Cause, confirmed. Chapters are saved by several paths: the reader, the background reading, the queue. When one of the others saves a chapter, the queue still counts it as “to go” (download_queue.dart:174), and the novel page isn't told it was saved (library_store.dart:968). Cancel empties the queue, the page refreshes, and the ticks suddenly appear. Jobs waiting up to 10 minutes after a site said “slow down” also show as a spinning “Downloading”.

Fix. The queue drops chapters already on the phone. A saved chapter tells the page straight away. The line shows “3 of 10”, and “Waiting to retry (site busy)” without a spinner.

A4 Scrolled into a later chapter, tapped, and Play now starts there Done · 1.4.2

Cause, confirmed. This is how it was built, and tests pin it. While the voice is stopped, scrolling by hand moves your place when the scroll ends (reader_screen.dart:1121). The tap only stopped the fling, so it looks as if the tap did it. “Stopped” is also true more often than you'd think: after opening any chapter, after Next chapter while paused, and after the voice moves into a new window.

Fix: needs your decision. My recommendation: once a book has been listened to, scrolling only looks around, and the “Back to reading” pill shows. Only “Read from here” moves the place. Paused counts as listening too.

A5 Reading stops at a chapter change after a while Done · 1.4.2

Two causes found by reading the code; likely.

  1. Going into the next chapter, it fetches three chapters ahead. If the second or third fails, it stops the whole reading, even though the next chapter came down. It then says “That is the latest chapter so far”, which is wrong (listening_session.dart:713).
  2. If you open the novel, or touch Contents or Next, while the voice is between chapters (fetching), the new page stops the voice instead of carrying it on (reader_controller.dart:91).

Fix. Fetch only the next chapter, and retry it. Count “between chapters” as playing.

A6 “The focus while changing chapters” Done · 1.4.2

Probably A1 and A4 seen from inside the reader. One more cause: when the voice rolls into a new set of chapters, the page is rebuilt at the voice and drops where you were looking. I'll ask what you meant.

4. How the voice sounds

B1 “...” makes the voice speed up Done · 1.5.0

Nothing handles this today. “...” followed by a capital cuts the sentence in two, so “I... I don't know” makes a tiny unit “I...” that the voice rushes. The single character “…” never cuts, so books that use one or the other read differently. “. . .” with spaces makes empty units with pauses. The 300 ms ellipsis pause only fires in narrow cases.

Fix: an ellipsis rule. Every form (..., …, . . ., ....) becomes one mark, read as a held breath: the voice keeps going and a set pause is added (Settings → Reading rules, on by default, with the pause length adjustable). It also stops cutting sentences at ellipses inside speech, turns -- into a dash, and folds !!! and ?!? into one mark.

B2 Natural voices go silent for 0.2–0.3 s mid-sentence Done · 1.5.0

Cause, confirmed in code. It matches your description exactly. Any sentence over 90 characters is cut at its first comma after about 40 characters, and each piece is made separately (neural_voices.dart:1162). At the join, the piece keeps up to 180 ms of the voice's own tail, and then the comma pause (150 ms) is added on top. That is about 230–345 ms of silence after 7–8 words, every long sentence. The cut exists only so a jump starts faster.

Fix. Cut only very long sentences (over ~200 characters). At a cut, never stack the comma pause on the voice's own. For natural voices, top the pause up to a target length instead of always adding.

B3 Speed swings, extra pauses, two words run together Done · 1.5.0

The same separate pieces (B1, B2, and a quote split from its narration) are each timed by the voice on its own, so short ones come out fast and long ones slow. When a quote and its narration are joined, each part's own padding is never trimmed, so a phone voice leaves 100–300 ms at every quote edge. A natural voice with no padding runs straight into the next part. A plain hyphen counts as a dash, so “well-known” can get a pause in the middle.

Fix. Trim each part, then put one set gap between parts. A hyphen is a dash only with spaces around it.

B4 The word is lit sometimes; other times only the sentence Done · 1.5.0

Cause, confirmed. A word is lit only when its timing is known, not guessed. Natural voices find word timing from the small pauses in the audio. A pause must be 70 ms long and 40 dB below the peak, and Kokoro and Piper rarely dip that far at commas. Any stretch longer than 3.5 s without such a pause loses word lighting. With phone voices, short sentences sometimes give timings the code can't read, and it then keeps the guessed timings instead of working them out from the audio.

Fix. Use a looser pause rule, allow longer stretches, and fall back to the audio when the phone's timings are unclear. A guessed word gets a soft highlight rather than none. A debug count per chapter (words lit vs. guessed) will prove it.

5. Smoothness (frames)

C1 Hiding or bringing back the player lags, worst while playing Done · 1.5.0

Cause, high confidence. In scroll mode the bars fold by changing their height over 220 ms, so the text area changes size on every frame. Each frame rebuilds about five screens of paragraphs and redoes the fade mask (reader_chrome.dart:233, reader_screen.dart:2484). During playback, the glow and word repaints add to it.

Fix. Lay the bars over the text, as pages already do, sliding and fading without changing the text's size.

C2 Following the voice (scroll and page turns) skips frames Done · 1.5.0

  • Scroll: it waits until the sentence is near the bottom (90%), then jumps up to three quarters of a screen in 360 ms. That is 90–180 px a frame, which looks like dropped frames even when none are dropped. Fix: follow sooner with shorter, gentler moves, and start the scroll on the frame after the sentence change, not the same one.
  • Curl pages: every frame of a turn rebuilds all three leaves, works out their accessibility text, and draws a blurred shadow. The leaves are also torn down and rebuilt at the start and end of each turn. Every word repaints the whole page. At a new chapter, the whole chapter is cut into pages in one frame. Fix: build the leaves once per turn, use a gradient shadow, repaint only the spoken line, and cut chapters a little per frame.
  • To check: the phone may drop to 60 Hz when you aren't touching it, so turns driven by the voice may run at half rate. Measurable; if so, ask for 120 Hz while reading.

C3 The constant lag Done · 1.5.0

  • The voice glow never stops. While the voice reads, it asks for a frame about every 33 ms, even when it is hidden or covered (voice_glow.dart:151). That is about 30 full-screen redraws a second, for nothing.
  • A full-screen fade mask over the text adds an extra render pass on every frame.
  • Each spoken word re-lays out the whole paragraph in scroll mode.
  • Now Playing's blurred cover is blurred again on every frame.

Fix: the glow stops when it can't be seen; two thin gradient strips replace the mask; the word is painted behind fixed text; the cover is blurred once.

Each fix gets measured before and after on your phone (frame times from SurfaceFlinger), as before.

8. Quick additions

Paradee voice

Paradee-8M (Hugging Face sahilmahendrakar/Paradee-8M-v1.2, Apache-2.0, released late October) is an English voice distilled from Kokoro, with 4 voices (af_heart, bf_isabella, am_puck, bm_fable). It is 8.5 MB (int8) against Kokoro's 334 MB, and its quality scores (UTMOS) sit close to Kokoro's.

The catch: it is not drop-in. Its ONNX inputs differ from what sherpa-onnx's Kokoro support expects (a voice number instead of a style vector), and sherpa-onnx doesn't support it. Two ways:

  1. ask the sherpa-onnx project to support it, which costs us nothing;
  2. run it ourselves with ONNX Runtime and a phonemiser in Kokoro's format.

Option 2 is a few days' work but feasible. Recommendation: open the request now, then build option 2 if there's no answer in a couple of weeks. A tiny, Kokoro-like English voice is worth having for phones too slow for Kokoro.

10. The reading helper (AI)

From your ChatGPT chat (“Build Shadow Slave AI”): the goal is an assistant that answers factually with chapter citations, can theorise with you across hundreds of chapters, takes new chapters of an ongoing novel, and never spoils past where you are. The chat proposed a server with Postgres, Neo4j and GraphRAG. That is right for a research project and far too heavy for a phone app.

A phone-sized version, free to run:

  1. Notes kept as you read, on the phone. When a chapter is finished, one small AI call writes a short summary and a list of who and what appeared (names, aliases, places). These are saved with the book. That gives a running memory of the story up to your place, never past it, so spoiler safety comes from how it's built.
  2. Recap where you left off, read aloud in your voice: built from the last few chapter notes. Costs almost nothing.
  3. “Who is this?” on a held name: answered from the notes, with the chapters where the name appeared.
  4. Ask the book (theory mode later): find the matching chapters on the phone (word search over the text and notes, no server), and send only those passages, with chapter numbers, to the AI. The answer cites chapters.
  5. The AI: your own key. Gemini's free tier fits best: a huge context, and free within daily limits, though Google may use free-tier data for training. OpenRouter is the “any model” option. A key is stored in Android's keystore. No TomeVoice server, so nothing to host or pay for.

Before designing it, I'll ask you about the qualities you want (you asked for this first). That comes in the next questions round, not this one.

Note: with this feature, IzzyOnDroid is ruled out twice over (section 1). F-Droid would list it under its “non-free network services” flag, which is allowed.

12. Z-Library and file formats

I recommend not building Z-Library in.

  • It is a criminally indicted piracy service (US case of November 2022), its domains are seized again and again, and it has no official API. It now works through personal login domains, Tor, and mirrors that users run themselves.
  • A built-in Z-Library browser invites a DMCA takedown of the GitHub repository, which takes the forks with it and would end the open-source project.
  • F-Droid refuses apps that infringe copyright.
  • Anna's Archive is in the same position, with court orders against it in 2026.

The app already lets anyone add an OPDS catalogue by URL. That keeps TomeVoice neutral, the way KOReader and Moon+ do it.

Formats. Z-Library's format filter lists TXT, PDF, FB2, EPUB, LIT, MOBI, RTF, DJVU, AZW and AZW3. TomeVoice already reads all of them except DJVU and LIT. Old Word .doc, CHM and comics (CBZ/CBR) are also missing.

  • AZW3 / MOBI / AZW / PRC: supported, as long as the file has no DRM.
  • DJVU: worth adding. Many scanned books come in it. It needs a DjVu decoder (djvulibre, GPL, native) to read the hidden text layer or show the pages for OCR. Medium-sized job.
  • .doc (old Word): medium. Its text can be pulled out without a big library.
  • LIT, CHM: rare; CHM is simple HTML in a container (small), LIT is an old Microsoft format (skip).
  • CBZ/CBR: comics are pictures. Only useful with OCR. Low priority.

Also found: Project Gutenberg plans to retire its current catalogue feed (XML OPDS) in 2027 in favour of OPDS 2 (JSON). Our Gutenberg catalogue needs to move before then.

15. The reading journal, the helper and memory (brainstorm)

The journal Agreed · built

  • Many entries a book, each pinned to where the story had reached when you put the pen down (Done): the chapter the voice is reading, or your reading place when nothing plays. Written while the voice moved on, it keeps the range too (“written while listening to chapters 4–6”). Shown as “Thoughts up to chapter 6” on the book's notebook page, among its quotes.
  • Two ways to write: a template of questions, or a raw page with none.
  • The default questions: How am I feeling? (Loving it, Good, Mixed, Meh, Frustrated, or a word of your own); Why?; What stood out?; What do I think happens next? Each can be skipped. Settings has a template editor: rename, add, reorder or remove questions, and choose for each free text or quick picks.
  • In backups and the Markdown export, and readable by the helper, which can say “at chapter 6 you guessed X” without mixing old guesses with what was revealed since.
  • Opened from a pen on the player (your pick), a book's ⋮ menu (Write in the journal) and the Journal row on its page.
  • The writing page (your pick): the notebook's squared paper with its spiral, the questions printed small, the answers in handwriting, the feelings as stickers to tap.
  • At a chapter's end (your pick): off by default; Settings can turn on a quiet “Write about chapter 6?” that goes by itself, the voice reading on.

Talking to an AI without leaving the app

ChatGPT Plus, Gemini Advanced and NotebookLM subscriptions cannot be used by another app; NotebookLM has no API for personal accounts. So: A. your own API key, the chat inside TomeVoice (recommended, and your earlier choice: a Gemini key from Google AI Studio has a free tier; OpenRouter or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint also work), with B. a hand-off beside it that sends a prepared pack (journal, notes, recap) to ChatGPT, Gemini or NotebookLM, for no setup at all.

Memory for a 3,200-chapter novel

Shadow Slave is around 11 million tokens: no AI can be sent all of it each time. Proposed, kept on the phone with each book:

  1. Chapter notes, made once as each chapter is passed: a short summary; who and what appeared (with aliases); facts revealed; titles, ranks and accolades gained (“Sunny: Sleeper → Awakened, ch. 210”).
  2. A ledger of people and things (the graph): each name, its aliases, and a timeline of its titles, accolades and ties, each with its chapter. This answers “what was revealed about X around chapter 1200”.
  3. Arc recaps every 25–50 chapters, rolled up, so the whole story so far fits in a few pages.
  4. Search on the phone over the chapters, the notes, your journal and quotes; only what matters is sent, and answers cite chapters.

Spoilers: nothing past your place is ever made into notes or sent. Catching up on chapters already read: one pass, about 20 chapters a request (around 160 requests for 3,200 chapters), resumable, on Wi-Fi while charging; roughly a dollar or two on a cheap model or a few days of a free tier (prices to check before building). Not recommended: raw-text search alone (weak on who-is-who and titles), or sending huge chunks to a long context (1M tokens is about 700 chapters).
